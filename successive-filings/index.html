The Stay in Successive Filings — § 362(c)(3) and (c)(4)

When the debtor has filed bankruptcy in the recent past, the automatic stay's duration and scope are limited under § 362(c)(3) and (c)(4). This page covers the special rules for repeat filers.

One prior dismissed case — § 362(c)(3)

If the debtor had a prior bankruptcy case that was dismissed within the year before the current filing, the automatic stay terminates 30 days after the petition unless the debtor files a motion to extend the stay before the 30 days run.

The motion must demonstrate good faith. The debtor must show:

  • The prior case was dismissed on grounds that don't preclude a current filing
  • The current filing is in good faith
  • The circumstances that led to the prior dismissal will not be repeated

If the debtor doesn't file the motion within 30 days, the stay terminates as to all creditors and all property. The case continues but without stay protection.

Two or more prior dismissed cases — § 362(c)(4)

If the debtor had TWO OR MORE bankruptcy cases dismissed within the year before the current filing, the automatic stay does not arise at all unless the court enters an order imposing a stay after notice and hearing. The debtor must file a motion within 30 days of the petition demonstrating that the filing is in good faith.

This is a substantial change from the default rule: the debtor must affirmatively obtain a stay rather than have it automatically.

Practical impact

For repeat filers, particularly pro-se debtors, the § 362(c) limits create a procedural trap. Many debtors don't realize the 30-day clock is running until creditors begin collection activity around day 25-28 of the case.

Common patterns:

  • Creditors who track multiple filings and time their collection resumption to the 30th day
  • Mortgage servicers in particular pay attention to these deadlines and resume foreclosure proceedings without notice if the stay isn't extended
  • IRS tax-refund offset systems may resume after the 30 days run

The good-faith showing

Good faith for § 362(c)(3) and (c)(4) purposes turns on:

  • Why the prior cases were dismissed (lack of payment, missed deadlines, technical defects vs. bad faith findings)
  • What has changed since the prior dismissals (new income, new circumstances, addressed issues)
  • Whether the current case is structurally different (chapter, plan terms, debt mix)
  • Realistic prospects of completion of the current case

Cross-reference

The automatic-stay rules for successive filings interact with the § 1328(f) discharge bar. Both apply to repeat filers but at different stages of the case. See the § 1328(f) research project for detail on the discharge-eligibility rules.
