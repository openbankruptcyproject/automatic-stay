Relief from Stay — Motion Practice Under § 362(d)

A creditor seeking to proceed with collection or enforcement against estate property must obtain relief from the automatic stay by motion under § 362(d). This page covers the four grounds for relief and the procedural framework.

Four grounds for relief

Section 362(d) provides four grounds on which the court can grant relief from the stay:

(d)(1) — for cause

"Cause" is undefined in the statute and is a totality-of-circumstances determination. Common cause grounds:

  • Lack of adequate protection of an interest in property
  • Bad faith filing
  • Unnecessary delay
  • Need to allow non-bankruptcy litigation to proceed

(d)(2) — lack of equity + property not necessary for reorganization

Two-part test: (a) the debtor lacks equity in the property, and (b) the property is not necessary for an effective reorganization. If both apply, relief is mandatory unless the property is necessary for an effective reorganization that is in prospect.

(d)(3) — single-asset real estate

Specific to single-asset real estate (SARE) cases (§ 101(51B)). Relief is available unless the debtor either filed a plan with reasonable possibility of confirmation or began making interest payments within specific deadlines.

(d)(4) — in rem relief

Allows relief from stay in rem (against the property itself) for two years if the court finds the filing was part of a scheme to delay, hinder, or defraud creditors, with serial bankruptcy filings or fraudulent transfers.

Procedural framework

Section 362(e) and Rule 4001 govern procedure:

  • Notice and hearing required — typically expedited
  • Preliminary hearing within 30 days of motion filing
  • Final hearing within 30 days of preliminary (60 days total from motion filing)
  • Stay terminates automatically if hearing not concluded within 60 days unless court extends "for good cause"

Adequate protection

Where the basis for relief is lack of adequate protection of a creditor's interest, the debtor or trustee may offer adequate protection (cash payments, replacement liens, additional collateral, indubitable equivalent) to defeat the motion. Adequate protection is governed by § 361.

Procedural traps

  • Local rules vary substantially on motion form, supporting evidence, and hearing format
  • Some districts require pre-motion certification by counsel of attempts to resolve
  • Failure to attend the hearing typically results in default grant of relief
  • Stipulations among debtor / creditor / trustee can resolve relief without formal hearing
