The Automatic Stay — 11 U.S.C. § 362

The automatic stay is the bankruptcy law mechanism that immediately halts most creditor collection activity upon a bankruptcy petition's filing. This research project provides a plain-language reference to the stay's scope, exceptions, modification mechanics, damages framework, and special rules for successive filings.

The basic mechanism

Section 362(a) imposes a stay against:

  • Commencement or continuation of judicial, administrative, or other actions or proceedings against the debtor that arose pre-petition
  • Enforcement of pre-petition judgments against the debtor or estate property
  • Acts to obtain possession of estate property or to exercise control over it
  • Acts to create, perfect, or enforce liens against estate property
  • Acts to collect, assess, or recover claims against the debtor that arose pre-petition
  • Set-off of pre-petition mutual obligations
  • Tax court proceedings concerning the debtor (subject to specific exceptions)

Why this matters

The stay is one of the most powerful protections in U.S. law. It is automatic — it applies the moment the petition is filed, without further court action, and it binds creditors regardless of whether they had notice. Violation of the stay can subject the violator to damages including attorney's fees and, in egregious cases, punitive damages.
