Scope of the Automatic Stay

The stay applies broadly to virtually all creditor collection activity. This page covers the scope categories under § 362(a) with practical examples for each.

Eight categories of stayed activity

Section 362(a) enumerates eight categories. The eight, with examples:

1. Commencement or continuation of actions or proceedings

Any pre-petition lawsuit against the debtor is stayed. The plaintiff cannot file new actions, take depositions, or seek discovery. Pending motions in pre-petition cases are stayed. State court trials with the debtor as defendant are stayed.

2. Enforcement of pre-petition judgments

Garnishment of wages, levy on bank accounts, sheriff's sales, registration of judgments — all stayed. A judgment obtained pre-petition is essentially frozen until the bankruptcy concludes.

3. Acts to obtain possession of estate property

Repossession of vehicles, eviction proceedings affecting estate property, foreclosure, lock-out actions — all stayed unless the property is not estate property or an exception applies.

4. Lien creation or perfection

UCC filings, deed-of-trust recordation against debtor's real estate, construction lien filings — all stayed for liens covering pre-petition obligations. Liens with effective dates pre-petition that are first perfected post-petition are typically void as stay violations.

5. Acts to collect debts arising pre-petition

Demand letters, dunning calls, automated billing reminders, credit-reporting status updates designed to collect — all stayed. The line between "informational" and "collection" can be subtle; courts have generally read collection broadly.

6. Set-off of pre-petition mutual obligations

If a bank holds the debtor's deposit account and is also owed a debt, the bank cannot offset the deposit against the debt without obtaining stay relief. This particularly affects bank set-off, IRS tax-refund setoffs, and similar mutual-obligation scenarios.

7. Tax court proceedings

U.S. Tax Court proceedings concerning the debtor's tax liability are stayed (with specific exceptions for petition filing).

8. Continuation of an action against a tax claim deficiency

Specific to tax-deficiency proceedings already in progress.

What is NOT stayed (overview)

Section 362(b) contains a long list of exceptions. The largest categories:

  • Criminal proceedings against the debtor (§ 362(b)(1))
  • Establishment of paternity, child custody and visitation, certain domestic violence orders (§ 362(b)(2))
  • Acts to collect domestic support obligations (DSOs) from non-estate property
  • Police and regulatory power actions of governmental units
  • Tax assessment and certain demand for tax returns
  • Specific securities and commodities settlement processes
