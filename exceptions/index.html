§ 362(b) — Exceptions to the Stay

Section 362(b) lists 28+ specific exceptions to the automatic stay. This page covers the categories with practical impact in the most common case types.

Criminal proceedings (§ 362(b)(1))

Criminal cases against the debtor proceed despite the bankruptcy. The reasoning: bankruptcy should not be a tool for shielding from criminal accountability. Note that this includes federal criminal proceedings (e.g., DOJ prosecutions for fraud) where civil collection is stayed but the prosecution continues.

Domestic support, custody, and divorce (§ 362(b)(2))

Several exceptions apply to family-court matters:

  • Establishment of paternity
  • Establishment of child custody and visitation
  • Domestic violence protective orders
  • Collection of domestic support obligations from property that is not property of the estate

Police and regulatory power (§ 362(b)(4))

Governmental units (federal, state, local, tribal) can continue to exercise their police and regulatory power despite the stay. Examples:

  • Environmental cleanup orders
  • Workplace safety enforcement
  • Securities-fraud civil enforcement
  • Antitrust enforcement
  • Tax assessment (but not collection — that's a separate exception)

The line between "police and regulatory" (excepted) and "money-judgment collection" (stayed) can be fuzzy. Courts tend to apply two tests: the "pecuniary purpose test" (is the action primarily about collecting money?) and the "public policy test" (does the action effectuate public policy beyond collection?).

Tax-related exceptions (§ 362(b)(9))

Tax assessment, demand for tax returns, audit, and notice of tax deficiency can proceed despite the stay. Tax collection (levy, garnishment, refund offset for non-tax debts) generally is stayed.

Securities and commodities (§ 362(b)(6), (b)(7))

Specific exceptions for securities-clearing-organization and commodity-broker financial settlements that need to proceed for systemic reasons.

Lessor recovery of nonresidential property (§ 362(b)(10))

If a nonresidential lease has been terminated pre-petition, the lessor can recover possession of the leased premises despite the bankruptcy filing. Residential leases have separate, more debtor-protective rules.

Educational accreditation (§ 362(b)(14))

Actions related to educational institution accreditation are not stayed.

Eviction by lessor under non-bankruptcy state-law judgment (§ 362(b)(22))

If the lessor obtained a judgment of possession pre-petition (under state landlord-tenant law), the eviction can proceed despite the bankruptcy — but the debtor can suspend this exception by certifying intent to cure under § 362(l).
