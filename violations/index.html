Stay Violations — § 362(k) Damages

An individual debtor injured by a willful violation of the stay can recover actual damages, including costs and attorney's fees, plus punitive damages in appropriate cases. This page covers the statutory framework.

The damages provision — § 362(k)(1)

"An individual injured by any willful violation of a stay provided by this section shall recover actual damages, including costs and attorneys' fees, and, in appropriate circumstances, may recover punitive damages."

Required elements

To recover, the debtor must establish:

  • The debtor is an individual. Corporate debtors are generally limited to civil contempt remedies for stay violations.
  • A stay violation occurred. Some act prohibited by § 362(a) was performed by the violator.
  • The violation was willful. "Willful" means the violator knew of the stay and intended the act — not that the violator intended to violate the stay specifically.
  • The debtor suffered injury. Actual harm, including out-of-pocket costs and emotional distress in some jurisdictions.

Categories of damages

  • Actual damages: direct out-of-pocket losses, lost wages from time spent dealing with the violation, costs of recovering wrongfully seized property, and similar.
  • Costs and attorney's fees: recoverable as a category separate from actual damages, available even where actual damages are minimal.
  • Emotional distress damages: available in many circuits where the debtor demonstrates significant emotional harm linked to the violation.
  • Punitive damages: available "in appropriate circumstances" — typically egregious violations, repeat conduct, or violations involving substantial harm.

Treasury Offset and similar federal-agency violations

Federal-agency violations (e.g., IRS tax-refund offset, Treasury Offset Program seizure of federal benefits) are subject to § 362(k) under sovereign-immunity-waiver provisions in § 106. Recovery against the federal government for stay violations is well-established in case law.

Pre-petition vs. post-petition violations

  • Pre-petition activity: generally not a stay violation (stay applies after petition).
  • Post-petition activity by creditor with knowledge: classic stay violation.
  • Post-petition activity by creditor without knowledge: still a stay violation, but the willfulness analysis turns on when knowledge was acquired and what the creditor did after.
  • Post-petition continuation of pre-petition activity: e.g., automated billing systems that continue sending dunning notices after petition. The creditor's knowledge of the petition is the key inquiry.

Procedural framework

Stay violation actions are brought via motion or adversary proceeding within the bankruptcy case. Most courts require a motion for actual damages and an adversary proceeding for punitive damages or substantial relief. Statute of limitations is governed by laches and the underlying bankruptcy case timeline.
